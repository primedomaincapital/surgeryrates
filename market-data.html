Surgery Rates: The Same Procedure, Three Different Prices

Ask what a surgery costs and you will get three answers: the billed charge, the amount an insurer allows, and the cash price for someone paying directly. They can differ by tens of thousands of dollars for the same operation in the same city. This page keeps those three apart, and shows why the setting — hospital inpatient, hospital outpatient, or ambulatory surgery center — moves the number as much as the procedure itself.

Methodology

Included: published 2026 cost compilations that aggregate CMS, Medicare and FAIR Health-derived figures, and a knee-replacement analysis reporting FAIR Health allowed-charge benchmarks by setting. No proprietary FAIR Health table is reproduced verbatim; figures attributed to FAIR Health are summaries as reported in the linked analysis. Excluded: individual hospital chargemaster lines presented as market prices; surgeon-fee-only quotes that omit facility and anesthesia charges; and any procedure average without a stated range, since the range is where the useful information sits.

What the numbers mean

Setting is the lever most patients never see. A knee replacement allowed at $15,000–$28,000 in an ambulatory surgery center against a $45,000 national average is not a different operation — it is the same operation without inpatient room charges and hospital overhead. The chargemaster comparison sharpens the point: list prices averaging 164% above negotiated rates mean the first number on a bill is the least informative one. For any elective procedure that can safely be done in an ambulatory setting, the facility question is worth asking before the surgeon question, because it moves the bill by more than most negotiations ever will.
